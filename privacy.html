Widgetly privacy policy

Last updated: November 14, 2026

Widgetly makes home screen widgets. It's built to keep your things on your phone.

What stays on your phone

  • Photos, GIFs and video clips you add are copied into Widgetly's private storage on your phone (a clip as still frames, without sound). They are never uploaded.
  • Designs, profiles and settings are stored on your phone only.
  • Step counts are read from your phone's step sensor and stored on your phone only.
  • Health data (Pro, only if you connect it): health widgets read your steps, distance, active calories, exercise time, sleep, resting heart rate and weight from Health Connect, the place on your phone where Google Health, Samsung Health, Oura and other apps keep them. Widgetly only reads the kinds you allow, only to show them on your widgets, and keeps the latest numbers on your phone. Health data never leaves your phone: it isn't sent to us or anyone, isn't in backups, and is never used for ads. You can change or remove access any time in Health Connect, or tap Disconnect in Widgetly's settings.
  • Calendar events are read from your phone's calendars to show on widgets you add them to. They are never copied off the phone.
  • Music: music widgets read the song title, artist and cover of what's playing, to show them. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere.
  • Backups and shared designs are files you choose to create and send. They go only where you send them.

What leaves your phone

  • Google Drive backup (Pro, only if you turn it on): Widgetly saves a backup of your designs, photos and widget settings to your own Google Drive, in a hidden folder that only Widgetly can open (Drive's "app data" folder). It uses your Drive storage. Widgetly asks Google only for access to that folder, never to your other files, and we never see the backups: they go straight from your phone to your Drive. Turning backup off stops new backups; to delete the old ones, remove Widgetly's data in Google Drive's settings (Manage apps).

  • Couple photos and doodles (Pro): a photo or doodle you send to your person is stored, small and in place of the last one you sent, in the same database as couple notes, where only the two paired phones can read it.

  • Friend groups: if you start or join a friend group, your chosen name, an anonymous ID, and the latest photo and caption you send are stored in Widgetly's Firebase database (Google), in place of the last one you sent, so the group's phones can show it. Only people in the group can see them. Leaving the group removes them.

  • Pets: a pet lives on your phone. If you raise it together with a code, the pet (its name, kind, needs, hat and scene), who looked after it last, and the names and anonymous IDs of the people raising it are stored in Widgetly's Firebase database (Google), so everyone's phones can show it. Only the people raising it can see it. So that your pets come back after you reinstall Widgetly or change phones, a copy of them with their coins, streak and items is also stored there under a random save code that only your phone knows (Google keeps that code with your phone's own backup); it holds no account, email or contact details, and each save replaces the last. If you sign in with Google on the pet's Settings tab (optional), the same copy is also kept as one small file in your own Google Drive, in the hidden folder only Widgetly can open, so signing in on another phone brings your pets back. That file is not on our servers; Stop saving stops it and Delete my save removes it. Your game scores stay on your phone unless you join the leaderboards: then the name you pick and your best score in each game are stored there for other players to see, until you leave the leaderboards. If you send your pet to visit someone you know, your name and your pet's name and kind are stored for them until your next visit replaces it. Pet reminders are made on your phone, not sent from a server.

  • Invites: to give you and your friends a free week of Pro, Widgetly stores in its Firebase database a scrambled code made from your phone's Android ID (a one-way hash: it can't be turned back into the ID, and it says nothing about who you are), your 6-letter invite code, an anonymous ID, and when an invite was used. It's used only to count friends who joined and to stop the same phone using an invite twice. If you were invited, Google Play tells Widgetly which invite link you came from.

  • Usage counts: Widgetly counts how many times a day features are used (for example "a friend photo was sent", "the Pro page was opened" or "the app crashed, with this kind of error"), added up across everyone, in Widgetly's Firebase database. The counts have no ID, name, photo or anything else about you or your phone.

  • Weather: if you add a weather widget, Widgetly sends the coordinates of the city you picked (not your location) to Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) to get the forecast. Searching for a city sends the name you type to Open-Meteo's geocoding service. See Open-Meteo's terms for how they handle requests.

  • Couple widgets: if you pair with someone, the hearts and notes you send, your chosen name and an anonymous ID are stored in Widgetly's Firebase database (Google) so your partner's phone can fetch them. Only the two paired phones can read them. Unpairing stops it.

  • Bug reports and ideas: if you send a report from Help, it includes what you typed (name, email and message) and details about the app and your phone to help fix problems: app version, phone model, Android version, language, screen size, which Widgetly widgets you use, which permissions Widgetly has, and, for bug reports, the technical details of the app's last crash. It never includes your photos, contacts, calendar events or anything shown on your widgets. Reports are delivered to the developer by email through Formspree (formspree.io), used only to answer you and fix the app. You can send two a day.

  • Theme links: when you send a theme as a link, the link holds only the theme's name, or for a theme made from your photo, its colors, font and quote style. The photo itself never leaves your phone. Opening a link loads a page on GitHub Pages (github.io), which, like any website, sees your IP address.

  • Ads (free version only): the free version shows ads from Google AdMob: a small banner on some screens, an occasional full-screen ad, and videos you choose to watch to unlock a theme. AdMob may collect your phone's advertising ID, approximate location from your IP address and how you interact with ads, to show and measure ads and prevent fraud, as described in Google's policy (policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites). In the EU, UK and Switzerland you're asked for consent first, and you can change it under Settings, Ad privacy choices. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings. Widgetly Pro has no ads, and nothing goes to AdMob.

  • Purchases: Widgetly Pro is bought through Google Play, which handles payment. Widgetly only learns whether you have Pro, never your payment details.

That's all. Widgetly has no accounts, and no analytics beyond the anonymous usage counts above.

Permissions

Each is asked for only when you turn on the feature that needs it:

  • Photos: through Android's photo picker, which shares only the photos you pick. Only if you use an "On this day" widget, Widgetly also asks to read your photos, to find ones taken on today's date in earlier years. They're shown on the widget and never copied or sent anywhere.
  • Calendar: to show your events on calendar widgets.
  • Physical activity: to count steps for step widgets.
  • Internet: for weather.
  • Notification access: only if you add a music widget. Android shares what's playing only with apps that have this access. Widgetly uses it for media controls only and never reads your notifications.
  • Usage access: only if you add a screen time or data use widget, to total today's screen time and data. It never leaves your phone.
  • Contacts: only if you add a birthdays widget, to read the birthdays saved in your contacts. Nothing is copied or sent anywhere.
  • Notifications: for to-do reminders you set, news about new themes and widgets, and a reminder before a free trial ends. Each kind can be turned off in Settings.
  • Apps on your phone: shortcut widgets list your apps so you can pick one to open.

Ratings

Once, after you've set up a few widgets, Widgetly may ask Google Play to show its rating card. Google Play shows it (or not) and handles your rating; Widgetly never sees it.

Deleting your data

Everything on your phone is deleted when you uninstall Widgetly. Leaving a shared pet removes you from it. To have your couple messages, a shared pet or a report you sent deleted, ask through Help in the app (or email widgetly.support@gmail.com) and they'll be deleted within 30 days.
